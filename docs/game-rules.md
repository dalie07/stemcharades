# Game rules

For players. One person explains, everyone else guesses.

## A round
1. The game picks the next explainer in turn and a card nobody in the room has seen yet.
2. Only the explainer sees the card: the term, formula or question, and a **Don't say** list.
3. The explainer types clues (online) or talks (Party mode). The clock is running: **60 or 90 seconds**, the host chooses.
4. Everyone else guesses.
5. The round ends when **everyone has guessed it**, when **time runs out**, or when the explainer **skips**.
6. Everyone sees the answer, the explanation and who earned points.

After the last round you see the final scoreboard and a list of the cards nobody got, for revision.

## Clues
Typed clues are checked by the game. A clue is **refused** (and nobody else sees it) if it contains:
- the answer, or a listed alternative answer, in any capital letters or plural form;
- any word on the **Don't say** list, in any capital letters or plural form;
- the answer split up to dodge the check, such as "photo synthesis" or "d n a";
- rude words.

Only whole words count: "molecule" is fine when the answer is "mole". Very short alternative answers such as
"he" or "ml" are not blocked as clue words, because that would block everyday English. Spoken clues in Party
mode run on trust.

## Guesses
A guess is **correct** if it matches the answer or a listed alternative, ignoring capital letters, spaces,
punctuation, a leading "the/a/an", and plural or singular. "DNA", "D.N.A." and "deoxyribonucleic acid" all count
for the DNA card.

A guess is **close** if it is one letter off (two for long answers): you get a "close!" hint, no points, and the
answer is not revealed. Short answers and numbers must be exact, so "RNA" is not close to "DNA" and "181" is not
close to "180". Everything else is **wrong**. You can keep guessing until you get it or time runs out.

## Points
| Who | Points |
|---|---|
| Guesser | **10** for a correct guess, plus **up to 10** for speed (falls evenly from 10 at the start to 0 at the time limit) |
| Explainer | **5** for each player who guesses correctly |
| Nobody guesses | Nobody scores |

Points are kept even if the explainer then skips. The explainer cannot guess their own card. A guesser scores
once per round.

## Choosing what to play
Choose as broadly or as narrowly as you like, in both Party mode and online rooms:

1. **General**: everything, across every level and subject.
2. **Level**: Primary, Secondary, University or Advanced. Pick one, several or all.
3. **Subject or course**: subjects at Primary and Secondary (Maths, Science, Physics…), courses at University and
   Advanced (Calculus, Organic Chemistry, Data Structures and Algorithms…).
4. **Topic**: inside a subject or course, e.g. Waves or Integration.

Every step is optional and allows several choices; stop at any step and play. Each choice shows how many cards it
holds, and parts with no cards yet are hidden. A choice can be shared as a link, e.g.
`/#/party?levels=2&courses=secondary-physics`, which opens straight onto it.

## Rooms
- The host gets a **5-character code** to share. Players join with a nickname, 2 to 12 players per room.
- The host picks what to play, the round length and the number of rounds, starts the game, and can remove a player.
- If you refresh, you get your seat, score and role back. If the host leaves, the role passes to the player who has
  been there longest.
- If the explainer is disconnected for **20 seconds**, the round is skipped and the next explainer starts.
- A room nobody touches for **30 minutes** is removed. If the server restarts, every game ends and you are told.

## Party mode
One phone, passed around. No account and no internet needed after the first load. Pick players, what to play,
round length and rounds. Each turn the explainer sees the card and taps **Got it** or **Skip** as
others guess aloud; the explainer earns 5 points for every **Got it**. After each turn you see the answers and
explanations, and at the end the scores plus the cards to revise.

## Fair play
The game decides the cards, the clock and the scores on the server. The answer is never sent to the guessers'
devices, so there is nothing to find by looking at the network traffic.
